# Infrastructure for Collective Agency

_A three-paragraph summary of a work-in-progress thesis · Ryan Olf_

---

Today, in spite of technical capability compounding faster than ever, people in developed countries no longer believe the future will be better than the present. The growing gap between what is possible and our experience of collective flourishing threatens the very idea of progress as a shared project. This is not merely a problem of perception. Flourishing in an ever-changing world requires _agency_: the ability to shape the future and adapt to change in accordance with what matters. Yet our ability to reason about where change is taking us, and to influence its direction, has not kept pace with its scale, complexity and speed. Science and technology widen the range of futures available to us; agency is how we choose the future we hope to thrive in and tailor it to meet our radically diverse needs. We have constructed a world of unfathomable possibility and peril, and we urgently need better tools to navigate it.

As technology simultaneously shrinks the world and makes it more complex, we depend more than ever on institutions to extend our agency and act on our behalf. But institutions are actors with agency of their own, and alignment between theirs and ours is not guaranteed. Institutional capabilities have grown faster than people's ability to shape their purposes and use, and the connection between institutional decisions and people's lives remains opaque. Even well-intentioned institutions struggle to recognise and correct misalignment when they are not legible to the people they serve: people cannot tell an institution where it is failing them if they cannot understand what it is doing or why. The feedback loop through which institutions become trustworthy instruments of collective agency is strained by the very forces that make them so essential.

In this work-in-progress thesis, I outline a vision for an _epistemic commons_—shared infrastructure where capabilities for collective reasoning can compound and help collective agency keep pace with technological and social change. In the commons, individuals and institutions would use AI-assisted interfaces to collaboratively build, inspect, challenge and adapt the models behind decisions that affect them, creating bottom-up legibility that informs personal choices while feeding local, lived knowledge back to strengthen institutional alignment and accountability. I'm sharing this vision to invite external feedback on open questions—spanning technical bottlenecks, real-world testbeds, privacy and anti-capture safeguards, and independent stewardship—that could shape the design of a potential ~£50m programme to catalyse a new era of human agency.
